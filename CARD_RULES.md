# Chef's Kiss Party Game: Card Writing Rulebook

**Version:** 17 October 2026 (updated the same day: Al approved the 6+1 hand) · **For:** anyone writing official Recipe Cards and Ingredient Cards, reviewing Cards from the Community, or tuning the Si's "Spice It Up" feature
**Built from:** the Laugh Engine tag taxonomy (test kit v1), 180 tagged sample cards (30 Recipe Cards, 150 Ingredient Cards, all Medium), and a blind test with 4 raters scoring the same 420 pairings each (1,680 ratings).

> **House term:** we say **Si**, never the other two-letter word. Use "Si" in every doc, UI string, tag and comment.

> **Al's direction (17 Oct 2026):** improving each card is the priority. **Si Spice It Up** is a key feature. Its tagline: *"The Si doesn't change your card, it pulls out the flavor."* **"Salt and Pepper"** is a candidate name for the button.

> **Approved 17 Oct 2026:** the new hand is **6 on-target cards + 1 random card**. The safe slot is dropped, and the wild slot becomes a random card. A safe-slot win is no longer an automatic dud (see Section 7).

## 0. The test in one table

Every pairing was rated **FUNNY** (you'd give it the Chef's Kiss), **OK** (makes sense, maybe a smile) or **DUD** (flat, too literal, or doesn't land). **Good = FUNNY + OK.**

| Group | Ratings | FUNNY | OK | Good | DUD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Laugh Engine hands (all slots)** | 840 | 24.8% | 46.1% | **71%** | **29%** |
| Engine, on-target slot | 600 | 26.5% | 49.0% | **76%** | **24%** |
| Engine, safe slot | 120 | 20.8% | 35.8% | 57% | **43%** |
| Engine, wild slot | 120 | 20.0% | 41.7% | 62% | **38%** |
| Random hands (same deck) | 840 | 21.7% | 47.4% | 69% | 31% |
| *Old benchmark: mechanic-only party game (about 30% good, 70% dud)* | | | | *~30%* | *~70%* |

**What it means for writers:**

1. **The writing did most of the work.** Even random deals from this deck reached 69% good, against about 30% for an evenly split, mechanic-only deck. Every card in this deck was written to be funny.
2. **The Laugh Engine helps when it aims.** On-target cards beat random by about 7 points on duds (24% vs 31%).
3. **The safe and wild slots hurt.** They pulled the engine's overall result down to barely above random (Section 7).
4. All 4 raters agreed on 43% of pairings. **48** pairings were 4/4 FUNNY, and **57** were 4/4 DUD. Those unanimous results are the examples used in this rulebook.

## 1. Humor principles in plain words

A Chef's Kiss happens when two cards **collide**: surprising at first, and it makes sense a second later. Five ways to make that happen:

### 1.1 Incongruity: surprise that clicks

Put together two things that don't belong, where the listener can still "get it" in a second. If there's no surprise, it's a dud (too literal). If it never clicks, it's also a dud (random noise).

- ✅ *The real reason the Wi-Fi is down* → **A cat knocking things off a table** (4/4 FUNNY). Unexpected, and instantly believable.
- ❌ *What really happens in the Bermuda Triangle* → **The ocean** (4/4 DUD). No surprise at all.

### 1.2 Benign violation: break a rule, safely

Something is funny when a rule is broken **and** it feels safe. The rule can be:

- **social:** manners, like burping or waving at the wrong person;
- **physical:** physics or bodies, like a sloth in a hurry;
- **logic:** category or cause and effect, like a goldfish with a law degree.

Nobody gets hurt, nobody is insulted, nothing is truly dangerous.

> **Test data:** cards that break **no** rule were rated DUD **38%** of the time. Cards that break a **logic** rule: **17%** dud. **Social**: 23%. **Physical**: 27%. *Break a rule.*

### 1.3 Status flips: raise the low, lower the high

Give a humble thing an important job, or catch an important thing being ordinary.

- Low raised high: **A raccoon in a tuxedo**, **The DMV** as *Your new middle name* (4/4 FUNNY).
- High brought low: **A celebrity chef who burns toast**, **A wizard who forgot his password** (4/4 FUNNY as *A terrible name for a superhero*).

### 1.4 Scale mismatch: wrong size, right spot

Put something tiny into a huge situation, or something huge into a tiny one. **The mismatch has to be inside the joke.** A big thing on its own is not funny.

- ✅ **A burrito the size of a bus**, **A fidget spinner the size of a car**: everyday things blown up.
- ✅ **The parking lot at the mall** as *What aliens think humans do for fun* (4/4 FUNNY): cosmic eyes looking at something dull.
- ❌ Plain cosmic and huge nouns flopped: **The sun**, **The ocean**, **A rainbow**, **Pollen season**, **The Pentagon** were among the lowest-scoring cards in the deck.

### 1.5 Fancy register for low things

Describe something lowly or gross in posh, official or luxury words, or put a fancy thing somewhere grubby.

- ✅ **Fancy cheese that smells like feet** as *What's actually inside the school mystery meat* (4/4 FUNNY).
- ✅ **A skunk at a perfume store** as *Something that would ruin a wedding* (4/4 FUNNY).
- ✅ **The DMV** as *A rejected flavor of ice cream* (4/4 FUNNY): official-sounding meets dessert.

## 2. The formulas

### 2.1 A funny Ingredient Card

> **[a concrete thing you can picture] + [one twist that breaks a rule, flips status, mismatches scale, or mismatches register]**
> 3 to 7 words. A noun phrase, never a full sentence.

| Part | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Picturable core | A person, animal, thing, place or food someone can see in their head | *a sloth*, *a wizard*, *cold spaghetti* |
| One twist | Exactly one twist, and it must create tension | *…in a hurry*, *…who forgot his password* |
| Short | Readable aloud in one breath | **A sloth in a hurry** (5 words) |
| Open | Works with many Recipe Cards, not just one | **A glitter bomb** won with show-and-tell **and** sleepovers |

**Two ways to win:**

- **Twist cards** (built-in contradiction). These had 24% dud ratings against 34% for plain nouns. Examples: **A sloth in a hurry**, **A wizard who forgot his password**, **A crossing guard with superpowers**, **A llama that spits compliments**.
- **"Everybody knows it" cards.** A plain noun works only when it is a universally shared, low-status, slightly embarrassing thing everyone has lived through. Examples: **Burping the alphabet** (the top card in the test), **A rubber chicken**, **The DMV**, **Mom's meatloaf**, **A TV remote that's always missing**.

**Weak twists don't count.** A preference or a mild fact is not tension.

- **A queen who loves bowling** scored low, because liking bowling breaks no rule.
- **A penguin who wants to fly** came out sad, not funny (wholesome + longing).
- Compare **A vampire who's afraid of the dark**, where the twist contradicts what the thing *is*.

### 2.2 A funny Recipe Card

> **[a frame that turns any noun into a new role] + [a "wrong" word: worst, terrible, rejected, secretly, really, newest]**
> It must accept a noun-phrase Ingredient Card grammatically. It must leave room for a collision, not a correct answer.

| Rule | Why (test evidence) |
|---|---|
| **Make it a role or label frame.** "A terrible name for a superhero", "Your new middle name", "The newest Olympic sport". | These were the 3 highest-scoring Recipes. Any noun dropped into them becomes something it isn't (a status or category flip). |
| **Use a "wrong" word.** worst, terrible, rejected, useless, secretly, really, actually. | It invites the clash and lets weird cards "make sense". |
| **Don't write a container Recipe** ("Something you'd find in…"). | *Something you'd find in a royal palace* was the 2nd-worst Recipe. It invites literal fits (a royal butler: 4/4 DUD). |
| **Don't ask for speech** if the cards are nouns. | *Something you should never say on a first day at a new school* was the worst Recipe. Noun cards don't fit "say". |
| **Don't stack absurd on absurd.** | *The world's most useless invention* (tagged absurd) scored low. When the Recipe is already absurd, nothing clashes. |
| **Avoid soft, wholesome, positive frames.** | *Grandma's secret talent* and *The best thing about being a grown-up* scored in the bottom five. Without a "wrong" word there's no tension. |
| **Keep the Recipe's own tags plain** (everyday, casual or dry, `violation: none`). | A plain Recipe gives the Ingredient Card room to be the surprise. |

### 2.3 Funny targets (every Recipe Card must have them)

Each Recipe Card lists **2–3 funnyTargetCombos**. Each combo is a set of tags on **at least two axes** that **clash** with the Recipe's own tags. Use a **different** humor technique in each combo. Also list **literalTags**: the obvious, on-the-nose fits that the Laugh Engine should push down.

Real example from the deck:

```
Recipe: "The worst thing to bring to show-and-tell"
 own tags: event · nostalgic · everyday · kid · violation none · status low
 funnyTargetCombos:
 1. scale clash: subject nature|place + scale cosmic|huge
 2. register clash: tone gross_mild + register fancy
 3. status/logic: violation logic + status high
 literalTags: subject thing|animal, tone wholesome
```

Winners it produced: **The science fair volcano exploding** (4/4 FUNNY), **A glitter bomb** (4/4), **Burping the alphabet** (4/4).

> **Lesson:** a tag match is necessary, not enough. **Pollen season** matched funny targets for *Something you'd find in a royal palace* and still scored 4/4 DUD, because the card has no joke of its own. Write the joke into the card first, then tag it.

## 3. Tagging rules (every axis, every card)

**General rules:**

- Every card gets **at least 1 and at most 2 tags on every axis.**
- Tag what the card **is**, not what it could become in a pairing.
- When a card has a twist, **the twist decides the tags** (scale, register, violation, status), and the core noun decides the subject.
- Two taggers should agree. If they don't, the card is probably unclear: rewrite it before you tag it.

| Axis | Values | Rules | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **subject** | person, place, thing, event, nature, animal, food, pop_culture, feeling, action | The **head noun**. A raccoon in a tuxedo is `animal`, not `thing`. Use two only if both are central (**A celebrity chef who burns toast** = person + food). No brand names or real people in official cards; pop_culture means generic ideas (a viral dance). | Burping the alphabet = `action` |
| **tone** | silly, absurd, wholesome, awkward, dramatic, dry, gross_mild, nostalgic | The **main feeling**, at most 2. `absurd` means impossible in the real world. `silly` is goofy but possible. `gross_mild` must stay family-safe (burps, slime, socks). | A sloth in a hurry = `absurd` |
| **scale** | tiny, everyday, huge, cosmic | The size **after** the twist. | A burrito the size of a bus = `huge` |
| **register** | formal, casual, kid, slang, fancy, official_sounding | How the **words sound** read aloud, not what the thing is. | The DMV = `official_sounding`; A raccoon in a tuxedo = `fancy` |
| **violation** | none, social, physical, logic | The rule the **card itself** breaks. Official Ingredient Cards should rarely be `none` unless they are "everybody knows it" cards (38% dud). Every violation must be benign. | A goldfish with a law degree = `logic` |
| **status** | high, low, neutral | The **dignity** of the thing. Ingredient Cards should be `high` or `low`; save `neutral` for Recipes. The flip comes from the pairing: high cards in low Recipes and low cards in high Recipes. | A royal butler = `high`; a pigeon = `low` |

**Recipe-only fields:**

- **funnyTargetCombos:** 2–3 combos, each on 2+ axes, each a different technique (Section 2.3).
- **literalTags:** the obvious fits. For *The next big social media trend*, list `pop_culture` + `slang`, so **A viral dance challenge** (4/4 DUD) gets pushed down.
- **level:** Mild, Medium or Spicy, set by **both** the reading level and the cultural level. Take the higher of the two.

## 4. Do's and don'ts, with real results

All of these are real pairings from the blind test, at Medium level.

### Do

| Do | Real winner (4/4 FUNNY) |
|---|---|
| Build in a contradiction | *A terrible name for a superhero* → **A sloth in a hurry** |
| Catch the mighty being ordinary | *A terrible name for a superhero* → **A wizard who forgot his password** |
| Give a low thing a big job | *What the principal secretly does after school* → **A crossing guard with superpowers** |
| Use shared, slightly embarrassing life moments | *Something you should never say on a first day at a new school* → **Accidentally calling the teacher "Mom"** |
| Use family-safe gross with a twist | *What's actually inside the school mystery meat* → **Fancy cheese that smells like feet** |
| Put an authority figure where they don't belong | *The real reason the Wi-Fi is down* → **The principal** |
| Make the boring official | *A rejected flavor of ice cream* → **The DMV**; *Your new middle name* → **The DMV** |
| Turn a familiar annoyance into a sport | *The newest Olympic sport* → **A group project where nobody does anything** |
| Use a mischievous animal doing a known thing | *What's actually inside the school mystery meat* → **An angry squirrel** |
| Make cards that travel | **A glitter bomb** won with *show-and-tell* **and** *the most dramatic way to end a sleepover* |

### Don't

| Don't | Real dud (4/4 DUD) |
|---|---|
| Write the literal answer | *The next big social media trend* → **A viral dance challenge** |
| Echo the Recipe's own words | *The worst theme for a birthday party* → **A pet's birthday party**; *Something you'd find in a royal palace* → **A royal butler** |
| Use plain big nature | *What really happens in the Bermuda Triangle* → **The ocean**; *What's hiding in the teachers' lounge* → **The sun** |
| Use pretty, wholesome things with no edge | *The worst thing to find in your backpack* → **A rainbow** |
| Use huge institutions with no twist | *The worst job for a robot* → **The Pentagon** |
| Use mild, dull mishaps | *The scariest thing in the world (according to a cat)* → **Forgetting your locker combination** |
| Use "sad" twists | *How I survived middle school* → **A penguin who wants to fly** |
| Use cards whose joke depends on a second sentence | *The best thing about being a grown-up* → **A tornado of glitter** (no link to make it click) |
| Rely on scary or big events that don't connect | *A sign that your house is haunted* → **An alien invasion** |
| Count on tags alone | *What the dog really dreams about* → **Pollen season** (on-target slot, still 4/4 DUD) |

**Card-level scoreboard** (average across every appearance; FUNNY = 2, OK = 1, DUD = 0):

- **Top:** Burping the alphabet 2.00 · A rubber chicken 1.67 · A sloth in a hurry 1.67 · A glitter bomb 1.67 · A llama that spits compliments 1.67 · Mom's meatloaf 1.67 · A raccoon in a tuxedo 1.56 · A celebrity chef who burns toast 1.56 · A TV remote that's always missing 1.56.
- **Bottom:** Forgetting your locker combination 0.08 · The sun 0.12 · The Pentagon 0.17 · A family reunion 0.17 · A rainbow 0.17 · The ocean 0.20 · Tripping on the stage at graduation 0.25 · A snow day 0.31.

**Tag patterns that held across the deck:**

- **violation:** logic or social beats none (17–23% dud vs 38%).
- **scale:** tiny and everyday beat huge and cosmic as **plain** nouns.
- **register:** `formal` was the weakest register (avg 0.57). `slang` and `casual` were strongest.
- **subject:** `nature` and `event` were the weakest subjects; `thing`, `animal` and `food` were the strongest.

## 5. Writing for Mild, Medium and Spicy

All three levels are **family-friendly**. The level sets the **reading level** and the **cultural world**, not how "dirty" the card is. Hot and Hellfire are player-only adult Organic Cards and are **out of scope for official writing**.

| | **Mild** (elementary) | **Medium** (middle school) | **Spicy** (high school) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Words | 2–5 words, simple words a 2nd–4th grader can read aloud | 3–7 words | 3–8 words; wordplay and irony allowed |
| World | Home, pets, playground, cartoons, snacks, bedtime | School life, group projects, the cafeteria, siblings, early internet life | Driving tests, jobs, prom, college applications, group chats, adulting |
| Best techniques | Silly physical violations, animals doing people things, family-safe gross, scale | All five, especially status flips with teachers and parents, and awkward moments | Dry irony, official or fancy register, social awkwardness, self-aware status flips |
| Avoid | Sarcasm that needs explaining, fear, embarrassment that stings | Meanness about real groups, crushes made cruel | Anything sexual, drugs, alcohol, violence, insults. **Spicy is not adult.** |

**Examples.** Medium cards are real cards from the test deck. Mild and Spicy cards are **illustrative samples only**, not tested or final.

| Level | Recipe Card | Ingredient Cards |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** (sample) | "The worst thing to find in your cereal" | "A sneezing giraffe" · "Grandpa's fuzzy slippers" · "A dinosaur who's scared of bugs" |
| **Mild** (sample) | "A silly name for a new pet" | "Captain Burp" · "A worm in a party hat" · "Cold pancake" |
| **Medium** (tested) | "A terrible name for a superhero" | "A sloth in a hurry" · "A wizard who forgot his password" · "A celebrity chef who burns toast" |
| **Medium** (tested) | "The newest Olympic sport" | "A cat knocking things off a table" · "A TV remote that's always missing" · "Burping the alphabet" |
| **Spicy** (sample) | "The worst thing to say in a job interview" | "Your mom's voicemail greeting" · "A strongly worded email to the moon" · "Senioritis, officially diagnosed" |
| **Spicy** (sample) | "What your future self texts you at 3 a.m." | "Your parents discovering emojis" · "A group chat with the principal in it" · "A 3 a.m. existential crisis" |

**Level check before submitting a card:**

1. Could the youngest reader at this level read it aloud?
2. Would they know the reference?
3. Would a grandparent laugh, not wince?

If the answer to 1 or 2 is no, raise the level. If the answer to 3 is no, the card doesn't ship at any official level.

## 6. Si Spice It Up

*"The Si doesn't change your card, it pulls out the flavor."* (Button name candidate: **"Salt and Pepper"**.)

When a player types a plain Organic Card idea, the Si can offer funnier versions. **The Si only suggests. The player always chooses.** Al has made this a key feature. The Build Spec (Section 10.1) turns these rules into requirements and tests.

### 6.1 Rules the Si follows

1. **Trigger only when asked.** The player taps "Spice it up" during the plant step. Never rewrite silently.
2. **Offer about 3 versions**, each using a **different technique**, each **labelled** with that technique:
   - **Twist**: add a contradiction;
   - **Status flip**: raise the low or lower the high;
   - **Scale mismatch**: wrong size;
   - **Fancy talk**: fancy or official register for a low thing;
   - **Rule-break**: a benign social, physical or logic violation.
3. **Always show "Keep mine"** next to the suggestions, with the original text, as an equal choice. The player may also edit any suggestion.
4. **Keep the player's idea.** Every version must contain the player's core noun or person (their uncle stays their uncle).
5. **Follow the card formula** (Section 2): 3–8 words, picturable, one twist, a noun phrase for Ingredient Cards, a role or label frame for Recipe Cards.
6. **Suggest tags** for each version (all six axes), shown as one-tap chips the player can change.
7. **Stay family-friendly** at the player's level (Mild, Medium or Spicy) by default. Only when the player is writing in **their own opted-in Hot or Hellfire deck** may the Si write at that heat level, and **never above the deck's level**. The Si never raises the heat by itself.
8. **Never punch down.** No insults about real groups, appearance, disability, religion, race or gender. No real-person mockery in official-bound text. Real names are fine in a player's own private deck (e.g. "Uncle Bob").
9. **Never share.** Suggestions are made for the player's own card. Nothing is submitted to Cards from the Community unless the player separately chooses to harvest it.
10. **Fail quietly.** If the Si can't run (offline, older device), hide the button. The player can still write and keep their card.

### 6.2 Before and after

| Player typed | Version 1 | Version 2 | Version 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| *a dog* | **Status flip:** "A dog who thinks he's the mayor" | **Fancy talk:** "A dog in a three-piece suit filing taxes" | **Scale mismatch:** "A dog the size of a parking garage" |
| *homework* | **Status flip:** "Homework that grades *you*" | **Fancy talk:** "Homework, signed and notarized" | **Scale mismatch:** "A pile of homework visible from space" |
| *pizza* | **Rule-break:** "A pizza that demands a tip" | **Fancy talk:** "Artisanal gas-station pizza" | **Twist:** "A pizza that's shy about its toppings" |
| *Grandma* (Family Reunion deck) | **Status flip:** "Grandma, undefeated arm-wrestling champ" | **Twist:** "Grandma's glasses, missing since 1987" | **Fancy talk:** "Grandma's official casserole regulations" |
| *Uncle Bob dancing* (Wedding deck) | **Scale mismatch:** "Uncle Bob's dance moves, visible from space" | **Rule-break:** "Uncle Bob starting a conga line at the vows" | **Status flip:** "Uncle Bob, the wedding's head of security" |
| *a sock* | **Twist:** "A sock with abandonment issues" | **Fancy talk:** "A single sock, recently widowed" | **Status flip:** "The sock that runs the laundry room" |
| Recipe idea: *things at a wedding* | **Wrong word:** "The worst thing to say during the vows" | **Status flip:** "What the ring bearer is secretly planning" | **Role frame:** "A terrible name for the wedding cake" |

Each version shows its suggested tags. For example, "A dog who thinks he's the mayor" = animal · silly · everyday · official_sounding · logic · low.

## 7. Safe and wild slots: dropped (APPROVED 17 Oct 2026)

**Finding.** The 7-card hand mix (5 on-target + 1 safe + 1 wild) hurt the engine:

- The **safe slot** was rated DUD **43%** of the time, worse than a random card (31%).
- The **wild slot** was rated DUD **38%** of the time, also worse than random.
- On-target cards alone were **24%** dud. Without the two extra slots the engine would beat random by about 7 points instead of 2.
- The safe slot did produce a couple of unanimous winners (**A celebrity chef who burns toast**, **The kid who always says "actually"**), but only when the "safe" card was itself a funny twist card. Truly literal safe picks died (**A viral dance challenge**, **The ocean**, **The Pentagon**).

**Decision.** Al approved this on 17 Oct 2026, and the Build Spec has been updated to match (Section 8.3 hand algorithm, Section 8.5 dud metric):

- **New hand: 6 on-target cards + 1 random card.**
- **The safe slot is dropped.**
- **The wild slot becomes a random card.**
- **A safe-slot win is not an automatic dud.** Duds are judged only by the literal checks.

**The original recommendation from the test** (now adopted, except that the 7-on-target option was not chosen):

1. **Drop the safe slot.** A deliberately literal card mostly creates duds, and literal winners are exactly what the dud metric counts.
2. **Rework the wild slot** into a **random eligible card** (random beat wild: 31% vs 38% dud), or drop it too.
3. **New default to test:** 6 on-target (with subject diversity) + 1 random. Also test 7 on-target.
4. **Update the dud metric.** Stop counting "the winner was the safe slot" as an automatic dud. Use the literalTags and literal-flag checks only.
5. Re-run the blind test to confirm. The goal is an engine at ≤25% dud (≥75% good).

Status: **approved by Al, 17 Oct 2026.** The next blind test should confirm the 6+1 hand against random and report duds by slot.

## 8. Limitations

- **4 bot raters, not people.** The raters were automated stand-ins rating the same 420 pairings. They agreed fully on only 43% of pairings. Real Chefs have different tastes, inside jokes and timing.
- **An all-funny deck.** Every card was written to be funny, so random hands already scored 69% good. That says the writing works, but it hides how much the Laugh Engine helps. **We need a mixed-deck test** that includes plain, literal cards (like an evenly split, mechanic-only deck) to measure the engine fairly.
- **Medium level only.** Mild and Spicy are untested. Their examples in this rulebook are samples.
- **Small numbers per card.** Most cards appeared 3–6 times. Card-level rankings are a guide, not a verdict.
- **No play history.** Freshness and win history were neutral in this test, so they are untested.
- **Phrasing analysis is approximate.** "Twist vs plain" was sorted by a simple wording rule.
- **Next steps:**
  1. a mixed-deck test;
  2. Mild and Spicy decks;
  3. **real human tables** with mixed ages, using the pairings log in the live build;
  4. a re-test of the approved 6+1 hand (no safe or wild slots).

## Quick checklist for every official card

- [ ] Picturable, 3–7 words (Mild 2–5), noun phrase (Ingredient) or role/label frame with a "wrong" word (Recipe)
- [ ] One real twist (status, scale, register, or a benign rule-break), or a true "everybody knows it" moment
- [ ] Not the literal answer to any obvious Recipe; doesn't echo Recipe words
- [ ] Not a plain big nature or institution noun
- [ ] Tagged on all six axes (1–2 each); Recipe has 2–3 funnyTargetCombos + literalTags
- [ ] Level set by the higher of reading and culture; family-friendly at every level
- [ ] A grandparent would laugh, not wince
